> This final topic covers material from weeks 14 and 15, referencing the concepts of the renowned sociologist [**Göran Therborn**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ran_Therborn). It is divided into two main parts: **"The World and the Left"** and **"Dreams and Nightmares of the World's Middle Class"**.
### **1. The World and the Left**

This point focuses on the evolution of the Left from the 20th to the 21st century:

- **20th Century (The Golden Age):** The Left was anchored by the "working class" and "decolonization movements," establishing welfare states, labor rights, and gender equality.
    
- **The Turning Point:** In the late 20th century, the Left weakened due to the collapse of the Soviet Union and the rise of **"Neoliberalism,"** which emphasized free markets and reduced the role of the state.
    
- **The Left in the 21st Century:** * **New Challenges:** The world faces the climate crisis and skyrocketing inequality driven by financial capitalism.
    
    - **Climate Change Alternatives:** (The exam might ask about the differences)
        
        1. _Anti-capitalist/Degrowth:_ Argues that we must stop prioritizing economic growth to save the environment.
            
        2. _Green New Deal:_ State-led economic reform to make the economy environmentally friendly.
            
        3. _Green Capitalism:_ Letting market mechanisms and technology solve the problem themselves.

### 2. Dreams and Nightmares of the Middle Class (Global Middle Class)
This section analyzes how the middle class isn't as ideal as it seems:

**Contradiction**:
* On one hand (Dream): The World Bank sees the global middle class growing as a symbol of success and consumption.
* On the other hand (Nightmare): There is a phenomenon called "The Vanishing Middle Class," where the middle class in developed countries is disappearing or experiencing downward mobility.

**Characteristics of the modern middle class:**
* They lack the stability of previous generations and face "precarity" (uncertainty).
* The nightmare: It is the "fear of falling," debt burdens, and increased competition in a labor market being replaced by AI or corporate downsizing.

Political dimension: The middle class tends to be "cosmopolitan" (global citizens) in terms of lifestyle, but at the same time, they may turn toward far-right or nationalist ideologies if they feel their security is being threatened.

### **3. Keywords frequently appearing in exams (Therborn's Theory)**

- **Financialization:** The phenomenon where the economic system is dominated by the financial sector rather than the real production sector.
    
- **Middle-class Professionalism vs Profit:** The conflict between "professional ethics" (e.g., doctors, teachers) and "profit-oriented management."
    
- **Good Job:** The definition of the middle class is having a "stable and well-paying job," which is becoming increasingly rare today.
    
- **Sleepwalking into war:** A comparison suggesting that the modern world is heading toward conflict (e.g., US-China) just like the period before World War I, without even realizing it.
